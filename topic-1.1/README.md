# topic-1.1 — Understanding Social Engineering

## Big idea
The easiest way past a locked door is to convince someone to open it. Social engineering exploits instinct — trust, helpfulness, fear of consequences, urgency — not code.

## Lesson map (JuiceMind 1.1, ~50 min)

## Scenario for this unit
Brightwave Dental's receptionist gets a phone call from "their IT vendor." Transcript and logs are in `data/`:
- `data/transcript.txt` — the full call
- `data/reception-log.txt` — front-desk log with caller details
- `data/network-map.txt` — which device sits at which IP (you'll need this for the checkpoint)

## Key vocabulary
- **Pretexting** — inventing a believable role/story to obtain information
- **Urgency** — manufacturing time pressure to suppress critical thinking
- **Authority** — impersonating someone with perceived power (IT, police, boss)
- **Verification** — independently confirming identity through a *known* channel

## Guided notes prompt
While reading, answer in the margin: which instinct did each part of the caller's script target?

## From the deck (teacher notes, abridged)
- Cold-open: play the transcript audio-less — have students flag the exact sentence where they'd hang up.
- Misconception warning: students think social engineering = email phishing only. The Brightwave scenario is phone-based on purpose.
- Quick check answers: B, C, B — review the "urgency + authority combo" in Q2 before moving on.
- Pacing: hook 5m → notes 10m → scenario walkthrough 10m → quick check 5m → checkpoint intro 5m.
